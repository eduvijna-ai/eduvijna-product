## Snapshot

Ananya is a Class Teacher and Subject Teacher in a mid-sized CBSE affiliated school in a Tier-2 Indian city. She teaches Science to Grades 6–8 and is Class Teacher for 7-B (42 students). She cares deeply about her students, works long hours, and constantly feels behind on paperwork even when classroom teaching goes well.

She is not looking for “AI magic.”  
She is looking for **a calm, reliable way to prepare well, teach well, and follow up** without losing her evenings.

## Goals

### Professional goals

1. Complete the syllabus on time with enough revision before exams  
2. Make every class clear enough that average students can follow  
3. Identify weak students early and help them improve  
4. Create fair, board-aligned assessments  
5. Maintain accurate records (attendance, marks, homework)  
6. Communicate professionally with parents without constant WhatsApp chaos  
7. Grow as a teacher (new methods, subject depth, classroom management)

### Personal goals

1. Leave school without carrying guilt about unfinished evaluation  
2. Protect family evening time at least 3–4 nights a week  
3. Feel prepared before the first period — not improvising under stress  
4. Be recognised as competent and caring by students, parents, and principal  

### Success definition (in her words)

> “If I walk into class knowing exactly what I’m teaching, who needs help, and what I’ll collect today — and I still get home before I’m exhausted — that is a successful week.”

## Daily responsibilities

### Before school

- Review timetable and any substitute / notice changes  
- Finalise materials for the day’s periods  
- Pack notebooks to return, worksheets to distribute, registers  
- Mentally prepare for assembly duty / corridor duty if assigned  

### During school

- Morning assembly / duty as rostered  
- Deliver 4–6 periods (own subject + possible substitution)  
- Take attendance (class teacher periods / homeroom)  
- Explain concepts, run activities, manage behaviour  
- Address doubts — often rushed between bells  
- Give and check homework selectively  
- Conduct class tests / oral checks when scheduled  
- Handle unexpected events (absent co-teacher, visitor, discipline, illness)  
- Brief corridor conversations with parents who arrive unannounced  

### After school / evening

- Correct notebooks and test papers  
- Enter marks / update records  
- Message or call a few parents  
- Plan tomorrow’s lessons and photocopy/print needs  
- Prepare for staff meeting / PTM / exam duty when due  
- Occasional professional development modules or webinars  

### Invisible load

- Emotional labour for struggling or distressed students  
- Keeping peace between syllabus pressure and student readiness  
- Being the “default problem solver” for her class section  

---

## Biggest frustrations

1. **Preparation takes too long** — same lesson rebuilt every year with slight changes  
2. **Evaluation never ends** — stacks of notebooks and answer sheets  
3. **Mixed-ability classrooms** — one lesson cannot serve all 42 students equally  
4. **Fragmented tools** — WhatsApp groups, paper registers, Word files, YouTube, school ERP login that feels separate from teaching  
5. **Last-minute changes** — substitute periods, syllabus acceleration, sudden tests  
6. **Parent communication overload** — repetitive questions, tone misunderstandings, after-hours messages  
7. **Data entry duplication** — attendance or marks written on paper then typed again  
8. **Pressure without support** — expected to personalise learning with no time to do it  
9. **Unreliable content quality** — internet materials often not board-aligned or age-appropriate  
10. **Fear of looking unprepared** — especially during principal observation or inspection

## AI expectations

### What she wants AI to do

- Understand **one teaching intent** (“prepare tomorrow”) and produce the full kit — not make her open six tools  
- Remember how *she* teaches (language, difficulty, worksheet style, Bloom emphasis) — not reset every login  
- Already know her school, board, timetable, and policies — she should not re-type them  
- Draft tomorrow’s lesson plan aligned to her textbook chapter and board  
- Generate worksheets, quizzes, PPT/sketch notes, and homework at the right difficulty  
- Convert her notes / PDFs / previous papers into usable assessments  
- Help her **observe** who is lost in the period — not only mark papers later  
- Summarise who is weak on which concept after a test and feed the next prepare cycle  
- Draft parent messages in clear, respectful language (English + local language)  
- Suggest remediation tasks for specific students  
- Reduce repetitive typing without removing her judgement  

### What she does *not* want AI to do

- Send anything to students or parents without her approval  
- Invent syllabus topics her board does not require  
- Replace her relationship with the class  
- Produce long, flashy content she cannot finish in a 40-minute period  
- Expose student weaknesses publicly or carelessly  
- Create more dashboards she must “feed” daily  

### Trust conditions

1. Show source / rationale (chapter, learning outcome, difficulty)  
2. Let her edit everything  
3. Remember her school context (board, grade, subject, medium)  
4. Be consistent — not brilliant one day and wrong the next  
5. Work within real class time constraints

## Segments within the teacher persona

| Segment | Distinct need |
|---------|---------------|
| Class Teacher | Attendance, diary, parent comms, holistic student view |
| Subject Teacher (non-class) | Fast prep + assessment + marks for many sections |
| Exam-heavy senior teacher | Board pattern fidelity, large evaluation load |
| Early-career teacher | Scaffolding, exemplars, classroom management support |
| Low-connectivity / vernacular medium | Offline empathy, language support, simple flows |

Primary TLM-001 modelling centres on **Class + Subject Teacher (middle school)** as the densest journey; other segments inherit and specialise later.

---

## Implications for EduVijna

1. Optimise for **daily preparation and assessment loops**, not occasional wow demos  
2. Treat **approval** as a first-class product moment  
3. Unite teaching artefacts with school records to end dual entry  
4. Speak the language of periods, chapters, homework, PTM, and unit tests  
5. Earn trust through explainability and editability before automation depth

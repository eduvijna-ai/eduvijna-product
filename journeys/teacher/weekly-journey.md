# Teacher Weekly Journey

**ID:** JOURNEY-TEACHER-WEEKLY  
**Persona:** Ananya (Class + Subject Teacher)  
**Status:** Draft — TLM-001

---

## Purpose

Capture recurring weekly rhythms that daily maps miss: tests, meetings, planning blocks, correction cycles, and the Friday collapse / Sunday dread pattern.

---

## Weekly shape

| Day | Dominant theme |
|-----|----------------|
| Monday | Re-entry, assembly energy, set weekly tone |
| Tuesday–Thursday | Peak teaching + midweek assessments |
| Friday | Completion pressure, submission deadlines, fatigue |
| Saturday | Half-day / activity day / correction marathon (school-dependent) |
| Sunday | Silent planning debt + emotional recovery |

---

## Weekly activities

### 1. Weekly academic planning

**Trigger**  
Sunday evening or Monday free period; coordinator expectations; upcoming chapter boundaries.

**What she does**

- Map chapters/topics to periods available this week  
- Reserve slots for class test / practical  
- Note resource needs (lab, smart class, printouts)  
- Align with neighbouring teachers if subjects share projects  

**Pain**  
Plans break by Wednesday due to holidays, special assemblies, or lost periods. Re-planning is manual.

**Desired outcome**  
A week plan that survives 20% disruption and still hits learning goals.

---

### 2. Lesson material batching

**What she does**  
Prepare multiple worksheets/PPTs/activities in one sitting to reduce daily thrash.

**Pain**  
Batching requires uninterrupted time she rarely has. Quality drops when rushed on Sunday night.

---

### 3. Class tests & formative checks

**Typical weekly pattern**

- 1 planned short test in her subject, or  
- Continuous formative checks across sections  

**Work involved**

- Create paper  
- Print / write on board  
- Invigilate  
- Correct  
- Enter marks  
- Decide remediation  

**Pain**  
Creation + correction dominate evenings. Insights arrive too late to change next week’s teaching.

---

### 4. Homework pattern management

**What she does**

- Balance homework load across the week so students are not crushed on one night  
- Spot chronic non-submitters  
- Design weekend homework carefully (parent politics)

**Pain**  
She cannot see other teachers’ homework load easily. Students experience “pile-on Thursdays.”

---

### 5. Staff meetings

**Trigger**  
Weekly / fortnightly academic meeting, department meeting, or sudden briefing.

**What happens**

- Syllabus coverage status  
- Exam duty allocation  
- Event planning  
- Policy reminders  
- Occasionally useful pedagogy sharing  

**Pain**  
Meetings consume prep periods. Action items lack follow-through tools. Teachers leave with more tasks than clarity.

---

### 6. Peer collaboration

**What she does**

- Share question papers with co-teachers of parallel sections  
- Moderate difficulty so sections stay fair  
- Discuss specific students who struggle across subjects  

**Pain**  
Collaboration is hallway-based and uneven. Best materials stay stuck on one teacher’s pen drive.

---

### 7. Notebook / file correction cycles

**Weekly reality**  
She cannot correct every notebook every day. She rotates: one section deep-check, others spot-check.

**Pain**  
Parents assume all work is checked daily. Guilt and disputes follow.

---

### 8. Parent communication cadence

**Weekly pattern**

- Routine updates for a few students  
- Escalations for attendance / behaviour  
- Clarifications before Friday tests  

**Pain**  
No shared weekly digest; everything is ad hoc chat.

---

### 9. Personal admin & self

**What she does**

- Update lesson diary / teaching log if school requires  
- Submit leave applications / duty swaps  
- Prepare for next week’s special duties  

**Pain**  
Administrative compliance competes with teaching craft.

---

## Weekly emotional pattern

```text
Mon: resolve / hope
Tue–Wed: flow + overload
Thu: assessment anxiety
Fri: deadline sprint
Sat: catch-up or collapse
Sun: planning dread ↔ family guilt
```

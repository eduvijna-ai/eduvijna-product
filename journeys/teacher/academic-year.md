# Teacher Academic Year Journey

**ID:** JOURNEY-TEACHER-YEAR  
**Persona:** Ananya (Class + Subject Teacher)  
**Status:** Draft — TLM-001

---

## Purpose

Model the full academic year — from pre-session preparation through admissions ripple effects, teaching terms, exams, results, holidays, and year-end closure — as lived by a teacher.

---

## Year at a glance

```text
Mar–Apr   Closing prior year / result work / board duties (context-dependent)
Apr–May   New session planning, admissions flux, orientation
Jun–Sep   Term 1 teaching, formative cycle, first big assessments
Sep–Oct   Revision, half-yearly / term exams, evaluations
Oct–Nov   Post-exam remediation, events, festivals calendar chaos
Nov–Jan   Term 2 depth, pre-board pressure (for relevant grades)
Jan–Feb   Final revision, annual exams
Feb–Mar   Evaluation, results, report cards, handover, holidays planning
```

Exact months vary by board and state. The **phases** remain.

---

## Phase 1 — Year planning (before / at session start)

### What the teacher does

- Receive class and subject allocation (sometimes late)  
- Study new textbooks / reduced syllabus circulars  
- Draft yearly syllabus split across months  
- Plan assessment cadence (unit tests, notebooks, projects)  
- Prepare first fortnight of lessons to start strong  
- Set classroom norms and parent introduction message  
- Understand new students joining via admissions  

### Pain

- Allocation changes after she has planned  
- Syllabus uncertainty from late circulars  
- New students with unknown learning gaps  
- Pressure to look “ready” on Day 1  

### Desired outcome

A living year plan tied to school calendar, not a static document for inspection only.

---

## Phase 2 — Admissions & settling

### Teacher experience

Even if admissions is an office function, teachers feel it:

- New students enter mid-stream  
- Bridge courses requested  
- Seating, peer groups, and level variance shift  
- Parents of new admissions seek extra reassurance  

### Pain

No learning profile for incoming students. Teacher rediscovers gaps the hard way.

---

## Phase 3 — Core teaching terms

### What success looks like

- Steady pace with buffer for lost days  
- Regular formative insight  
- Students building confidence chapter by chapter  
- Manageable evaluation debt  

### Recurring sub-cycles

Embedded daily / weekly / monthly journeys (see sibling docs).

### Year-specific pressures

- Completing practicals / projects  
- Competition / olympiad interest from a few students  
- Maintaining parallel sections at comparable standard  
- Supporting students who miss long stretches (illness, travel, festivals)

---

## Phase 4 — Examinations

### Types teachers navigate

| Exam type | Teacher load |
|-----------|--------------|
| Periodic / unit tests | Create, conduct, correct, remediate |
| Term / half-yearly | Heavier papers, moderation, marks entry |
| Pre-boards (senior) | High anxiety; many practice papers |
| Board exams (duty) | Invigilation, evaluation camps (as assigned) |
| Practical exams | Setup, viva, internal marks integrity |

### Pain

- Paper setting under pattern constraints  
- Fairness across sections  
- Massive evaluation within short windows  
- Absentee re-tests  
- Result pressure conversations  

### Desired outcome

Exam seasons that are intense but operationally supported — not chaotic.

---

## Phase 5 — Results & reporting

### What she does

- Finalise internals  
- Cross-check totals  
- Write cumulative remarks  
- Explain results to parents  
- Handle recheck requests emotionally and procedurally  
- Reflect on what to change next year  

### Pain

Errors are costly. Insights for next cohort are rarely captured systematically.

---

## Phase 6 — Holidays & “holiday homework”

### What she does

- Design holiday homework that is meaningful, not punishment  
- Sometimes create remedial holiday packs for weak students  
- Catch up on unfinished correction / planning  
- Attend mandatory trainings in vacation windows  
- Rest — if possible  

### Pain

Holiday homework becomes parent conflict. Teachers still work through breaks.

---

## Phase 7 — Handover & continuous improvement

### What she does

- Pass student notes to next year’s class teacher (informally)  
- Archive question papers and lesson files  
- Update personal “what worked” memory  
- Lose much of that memory by next April  

### Opportunity

Institutional memory of teaching that follows the class and the teacher — without extra bureaucracy.

## Yearly emotional arc

| Phase | Emotion |
|-------|---------|
| Allocation & Day 1 | Anxiety + resolve |
| First month | Hopeful exhaustion |
| Mid-term | Rhythm or backlog despair |
| Pre-exam | Pressure cooker |
| Post-result | Relief / disappointment / resolve |
| Vacation | Ambivalent rest |

## Academic-year opportunity themes

| Year job | Extraordinary help |
|----------|--------------------|
| Plan syllabus year | Calendar-aware year plan from board curriculum + school events |
| Onboard new students | Intake diagnostic + bridge plan |
| Run exam cycles | Paper generation, blueprint fidelity, marking assist, analytics |
| Close the year | Evidence-based remarks + next-year handover packs |
| Holiday learning | Differentiated holiday plans with parent-friendly instructions |
| Improve next year | Auto-capture of teacher edits and successful lessons as reusable kits |

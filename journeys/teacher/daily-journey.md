# Teacher Daily Journey

**ID:** JOURNEY-TEACHER-DAILY  
**Persona:** Ananya (Class + Subject Teacher)  
**Lens:** Lived day — not software screens  
**Status:** Draft — TLM-001

---

## Journey purpose

Map everything a teacher experiences in a typical school day: before school, during school, and after school. Identify moments of stress, judgement, care, and wasted effort.

---

## Day shape (typical)

| Phase | Clock (indicative) | Energy |
|-------|--------------------|--------|
| Before school | 5:45 – 7:30 AM | Focused / rushed |
| Travel & arrival | 7:30 – 8:00 AM | Transitional |
| School day | 8:00 AM – 2:30/3:30 PM | High demand |
| After school on campus | 2:30 – 4:00 PM | Depleting |
| Evening at home | 6:00 – 10:00 PM | Split: family vs work |

Exact timings vary by school; the emotional sequence is stable.

---

## A. Before School

### A1. Waking into responsibility

**What happens**  
She wakes with the day’s timetable in mind — which classes, which pending notebooks, whether a test is due, whether she has assembly or corridor duty.

**Feeling**  
Anticipatory load. If yesterday’s evaluation is unfinished, guilt starts early.

**Value moment for EduVijna**  
A calm overnight brief: “Today’s periods, materials ready/not ready, students to watch.”

---

### A2. Planning (micro-planning for today)

**What happens**  
Even if weekly planning exists, mornings involve last confirmation:

- Which examples to use in Period 2  
- Whether the worksheet is printed  
- What homework to assign  
- How to compress content if yesterday spilled over  

**Current process**  
Mental checklist + scribbled sticky notes + flipping textbook.

**Pain**  
Planning quality depends on how tired she was last night.

---

### A3. Materials readiness

**What happens**

- Locate textbook, notebook stacks, worksheets, chalk/markers  
- Print or photocopy if the office opens early enough  
- Download a video if the smart class is booked  
- Charge phone; ensure attendance app/login works  

**Pain**  
Photocopier queues, missing prints, wrong version of worksheet, video won’t load on school network.

---

### A4. Travel

**What happens**  
Bus / scooter / shared ride. May reply to parent WhatsApp messages mid-commute. May mentally rehearse a difficult explanation.

**Pain**  
Work invades travel. Safety and attention conflict with “quick replies.”

---

## B. During School

### B1. Arrival & soft start

**What happens**  
Sign in / biometric, check notice board, hear corridor gossip about timetable changes, collect keys for lab/smart class, drop bags in staff room.

**Unexpected inputs**  
Substitute request, sudden meeting, student crisis waiting at the door.

---

### B2. Morning assembly

**What happens**  
If on duty: manage lines, discipline, announcements, national symbols, birthday mentions, principal remarks. If not on duty: stand with class, mark mental attendance, settle students.

**Teaching impact**  
Assembly overruns shrink Period 1. Announcements create new tasks (collect forms, remind fees, event practice).

---

### B3. Homeroom / first contact (Class Teacher)

**What happens**

- Attendance  
- Diary / uniform / homework compliance scan  
- Quick emotional read of the room  
- Collect leave notes  
- Pass school announcements  

**Pain**  
Attendance + behaviour + announcements compete with the urge to start subject teaching immediately.

---

### B4. Teaching periods (core loop)

Repeated 4–6 times daily with variation:

| Step | Teacher intent |
|------|----------------|
| Enter & settle | Claim attention quickly |
| Connect | Link to prior learning |
| Explain | Concept + examples |
| Check understanding | Questions, board work, peer explain |
| Practice | Classwork / activity |
| Assign | Homework / revise pointer |
| Close | Summarise; manage exit discipline |

**Hidden work inside a period**

- Adjust pace for blank faces  
- Handle latecomers and interruptions  
- Invent an example when the planned one fails  
- Keep high performers from boredom while supporting strugglers  
- Note privately who did not understand  

**Pain**  
40 minutes is short. Doubts spill into recess. Mixed ability makes one pitch imperfect for everyone.

---

### B5. Assessments (when scheduled)

**Types in a day**

- Oral questioning  
- Exit tickets / 5-question checks  
- Class test  
- Practical / activity rubric  
- Notebook correction during class (rare but real)

**Pain**  
Invigilation eats teaching time. Cheating management. Incomplete scripts from absentees create follow-up debt.

---

### B6. Doubts & remediation micro-moments

**What happens**  
Students ask after class, during recess, or in a whisper during practice. She gives a 90-second re-explanation or promises “see me after school.”

**Pain**  
The students who need help most may not ask. Those who ask may consume all spare minutes.

---

### B7. Homework cycle (daily)

**Assign** — write on board / diary / WhatsApp  
**Receive** — incomplete, copied, missing  
**Check** — selective or full, depending on energy  
**Record** — ticks, remarks, sometimes ERP  

**Pain**  
Homework without insight is theatre. Checking 42 notebooks properly is often impossible the same day.

---

### B8. Attendance (beyond morning)

**What happens**  
Period attendance for subject classes (in some schools), updates for late arrivals, coordination with office for long absences.

**Pain**  
Paper-to-digital double entry. Disputes when parents claim “present.”

---

### B9. Communication bursts

**Intra-day channels**

- Student face-to-face  
- Peer teachers (substitution, shared students)  
- Office / coordinator  
- Parent at gate or on phone  
- WhatsApp pings she tries to ignore until free period  

**Pain**  
Context switching destroys lesson flow. Tone risk on hasty replies.

---

### B10. Unexpected events (daily chaos tax)

Examples that rewrite the day:

- Co-teacher absent → extra period  
- Smart class cancelled → redesign on the fly  
- Student illness / injury  
- Discipline incident requiring write-up  
- Principal walk-in observation  
- Sudden circular: submit marks by EOD  
- Rainy day schedule / special assembly rehearsal  

**Capability need**  
Resilience aids: quick alternate activities, auto-adjusted plan for lost periods, draft incident notes.

---

### B11. Free / prep periods (if any)

**Ideal use**  
Correct books, prepare next class, call a parent, breathe.

**Actual use**  
Often consumed by cover periods, forms, or queue at the photocopier.

---

## C. After School

### C1. Campus wind-down

**What happens**  
Detention duty, club duty, sports escort, meeting spillover, packing notebooks into bags that weigh too much.

---

### C2. Evaluation

**What happens**

- Correct classwork / homework / tests  
- Write remarks  
- Tally scores  
- Identify who needs re-teach  

**Pain**  
Largest silent time sink. Quality of feedback declines as fatigue rises.

---

### C3. Reports & records

**What happens**  
Update marklists, complete attendance irregularities, fill remark banks for diaries, prepare notes for coordinator.

**Pain**  
Same data reshaped into multiple formats for office, ERP, and personal tracking.

---

### C4. Parent communication

**What happens**  
2–5 targeted messages/calls on a typical day; spikes near tests and PTM. Topics: absence, behaviour, missing work, performance worry, event consent.

**Pain**  
Emotional labour + after-hours expectation. Language and tone must be careful.

---

### C5. Planning tomorrow

**What happens**  
Decide learning objectives, examples, materials, homework. Adjust if today’s class did not finish.

**Pain**  
Planning starts when cognitive energy is lowest — unless morning adrenaline masks unpreparedness.

---

### C6. Professional development (intermittent evenings)

**What happens**  
Watch a pedagogy video, complete a mandatory school module, read circulars about exam pattern changes.

**Pain**  
PD competes with evaluation. Rarely feels immediately useful the next morning.

---

## Daily journey map (condensed)

```text
Wake → Micro-plan → Materials → Travel
  → Assembly → Homeroom/Attendance
  → Teach → Check learning → Assign
  → (repeat periods) → Interruptions
  → Evaluate → Record → Message parents
  → Plan tomorrow → Sleep (maybe)
```

---

## Emotional arc

| Moment | Emotion |
|--------|---------|
| Prepared first period | Confidence |
| Blank student faces | Self-doubt |
| Finished on-time syllabus beat | Relief |
| Notebook pile at 9 PM | Resentment / duty |
| Parent “thank you” message | Meaning |
| Unexpected cover period | Stress spike |

---

## Highest-leverage daily intervention points

1. **Night-before / morning prep pack** for each period  
2. **In-period understanding checks** that are fast to run and interpret  
3. **After-class weak-student shortlist** without full marking completion  
4. **Parent message drafts** from facts already in the system  
5. **Lost-period recovery plan** when the day breaks

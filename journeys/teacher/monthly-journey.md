# Teacher Monthly Journey

**ID:** JOURNEY-TEACHER-MONTHLY  
**Persona:** Ananya (Class + Subject Teacher)  
**Status:** Draft — TLM-001

---

## Purpose

Document monthly and near-monthly cycles: PTMs, report windows, unit tests, larger planning resets, and school events that reshape teaching life.

---

## Monthly rhythm (typical academic month)

| Week of month | Dominant load |
|---------------|---------------|
| Week 1 | Syllabus push after prior month’s tests; attendance regularisation |
| Week 2 | Steady teaching; activity / club peaks |
| Week 3 | Unit test / periodic assessment prep + conduct |
| Week 4 | Evaluation, marks entry, reports, PTM prep, recovery |

Schools differ; the **assess → report → meet parents → reset** loop is common.

---

## Monthly activities

### 1. Parent–Teacher Meeting (PTM)

**Trigger**  
School calendar; sometimes emergency mini-PTMs for weak students.

**Teacher work before PTM**

- Finalise marks and remarks  
- Rank concerns per student (academic / behavioural / attendance)  
- Prepare evidence: notebooks, test copies, anecdotes  
- Anticipate difficult conversations  
- Coordinate with subject teachers if she is class teacher  

**During PTM**

- 3–7 minutes per parent under crowd pressure  
- Translate marks into plain language  
- Give one actionable home suggestion  
- Manage emotion, blame, and negotiation  

**After PTM**

- Note commitments  
- Follow up on promised remediation  
- Handle parents who missed the slot via phone  

**Pain**  
Preparation is huge; conversation time is tiny; follow-up is unstructured.

**Desired outcome**  
Every parent leaves with clarity and one next step; teacher leaves with a tracked follow-up list — not mental residue.

---

### 2. Reports & remark writing

**What she does**

- Compute totals / grades  
- Write student report remarks (often for entire class if class teacher)  
- Fix discrepancies between subject entries  
- Meet office deadlines  

**Pain**  
Remark writing becomes copy-paste. Personalisation dies under volume. Errors create parent distrust.

---

### 3. Exam / unit test preparation month

**What she does**

- Identify must-finish topics  
- Build revision plan  
- Create practice papers  
- Conduct remedial classes (sometimes unpaid extra time)  
- Manage student anxiety and parent pressure  

**Pain**  
Revision collides with continuing new syllabus demands from pacing guides.

---

### 4. Attendance & continuous evaluation audits

**What she does**  
Reconcile registers, update continuous assessment components, respond to office queries on absentees.

**Pain**  
Audit fear causes conservative over-documentation.

---

### 5. Departmental academic review

**What she does**  
Present coverage %, average scores, weak topics; receive instructions to “improve.”

**Pain**  
Reviews ask for insight she has no time to compute manually.

---

### 6. School events layered on teaching

Examples: Sports Day practice, Annual Day rehearsals, interhouse competitions, celebration weeks, inspection drills.

**Impact**  
Lost periods, exhausted students, compressed syllabus later.

**Need**  
Automatic awareness that teaching plans must compress — not denial.

---

### 7. Professional requirements

- Submit lesson plans file for the month  
- Update portfolios for appraisal  
- Attend scheduled PD  

**Pain**  
Compliance artefacts diverge from actual teaching artefacts (duplicate work).

---

## Monthly pain concentration

1. Remark writing at scale  
2. PTM preparation without student-level narratives ready  
3. Marks reconciliation across formats  
4. Remediation promises without operational system  
5. Event-driven syllabus slip  

---

## Monthly opportunity themes

| Job | Extraordinary help |
|-----|--------------------|
| Prepare PTM | Per-student briefing card from attendance + assessments + homework patterns |
| Write reports | Draft remarks from evidence; teacher edits tone |
| Plan remediation month | Auto-group students by weak concepts; suggest 2-week plan |
| Survive events | Re-pace syllabus when calendar blocks teaching days |
| Department review | One-click coverage + performance narrative |
